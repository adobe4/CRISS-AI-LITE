## The 10-beat structure (every big hit follows it)
| # | Beat | Time (2:30 story) | Example from the hits |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The rule, in one sentence** | 0:00 | "In this town, everyone is only allowed to take 1 million steps in their entire life." |
| 2 | **"You see," + how it works + the cost** | 0:03 | "There is a glowing number on your chest… when it hits zero, your legs turn to stone." |
| 3 | **World montage: 3–5 funny ways the town adapts** | 0:08 | Wagons to school, grandmas on zip lines, "the mailman delivers on a pogo stick because a bounce only counts as one." |
| 4 | **"My name is…": the hero is different, or punished by the rule** | 0:20 | "Kids call me wheelbarrow boy." / "I'm the best fake sleeper alive." |
| 5 | **The PLANT: a skill, object or saying that matters later** | 0:30 | Handstands every night · grandma's locked scissors · the recess liar game · grandpa's music-box saying |
| 6 | **The stakes: a loved one taken or in danger** | 0:45 | Baby sister's stroller rolling to the river · best friend Theo pulled into the sky · dad late in a blizzard |
| 7 | **A countdown in numbers** | 1:00 | "300… 250… 200… 3, 2, 1, zero." / "11:50. 11:55. 11:59. 12:00." / "7 minutes. 8. 9. 10." |
| 8 | **The twist: the rule's own wording + the plant = the way out** | 1:20 | "The counter only counts steps your FEET take" (so he walks on his hands) · "it never takes sleepwalkers" · the villain has a string too, *snip* |
| 9 | **The payoff: the town changes** | 1:40 | The whole town now walks on their hands · the hero now holds the strings handle |
| 10 | **The button: one short last line, funny or creepy** | last 3 s | "Everyone except Theo." · "Recess." · "Did I miss the math test?" · "Your tie is red." |

## What the numbers say
- **Length:** the 28M–36M hits run **1:53 to 2:55**. The 1:05 stories got 2–3.6M. A 0:35 twist-only story got 4.4M. **Aim for 2:00–2:45.**
- **Pace:** about **3 words a second**, so 330–520 words. It's fast, with no pauses.
- **Voice:** one narrator, first person ("I"). For our channel, a teen or adult voice.
- **Sound words are written into the script:** "Tink, tink-a-tink." "Knock. Knock. Knock." "Snip!" "Crack." Commenters quote them (one comment about "the most aggressive snip" got 1.2K likes).
- **Titles:** 3–7 words plus ONE emoji. Gneef's biggest titles drop "In This Town" and name the strange thing instead: "Everyone Has A String On Their Back 🧶", "The Knocker Copies Your Voice ❄️". **The first spoken line always says "In this town" or "In this world".**
- **Description:** only hashtags: `#shorts #roblox #entertainment #viral #relatable`.

## Why the comments explode (and push the video)
1. **A loophole people can argue about.** "Why not use cars?" got 18K likes and 255 replies; "I would just leave town" got 1.5K. Leave one obvious "but why didn't they…" question open on purpose.
2. **A named side character who gets a funny moment.** "Except Theo 😭" got 20K likes, and Theo's dance got 8.4K.
3. **Readers doing maths or reasoning about the rule** (step counts, logic puzzles). Rules with numbers start comment threads.
4. **Recognition:** "This is based on the show From" got 979 likes, so familiar horror ideas get people talking.

## Looks (from the frames)
- **Vertical 9:16.** Blocky Roblox avatars in lit 3D scenes: a cozy house, a park, a school, a night street.
- **Word-by-word captions** in the lower middle. *For us: much BIGGER, following our no-small-words rule.*
- **Close-ups on faces** at emotional beats. **Rule signs as props** ("BY ORDER OF THE TOWN — NO SCISSORS").
- **Night scenes with coloured light** for spooky beats, warm light for the payoff.
- **Recurring look:** a hero with bright hair (orange or purple), so viewers recognise the channel.

## Tone that works for the widest audience
- **Gneef's mix** wins the biggest numbers (28M–36M): spooky monster + kid hero + clever win + cozy funny ending. **Our channel is for teens and adults (owner decision):** lean into thriller and horror, dark twists (R6Rants-style) and grown-up emotion. Keep it ad-safe: no gore or swearing.
- **R6Rants goes darker** (The Hollows ends with the dad gone): 6.7M, but a smaller ceiling.
- **Akira's Diary does short underdog lessons** (65 s): steady 2–3.6M.

## Checklist before a script is approved
- [ ] The rule fits in one sentence and makes you picture it instantly
- [ ] "You see," + how it works + a cost
- [ ] 3+ funny world details
- [ ] The hero has a planted skill, object or saying
- [ ] A loved one is in danger by the halfway point
- [ ] A countdown in numbers
- [ ] The twist uses the rule's exact wording + the plant
- [ ] The town changes at the end
- [ ] The last line is under 8 words and quotable
- [ ] One arguable loophole is left open (comment bait)
- [ ] A named side character gets one funny moment
- [ ] 2:00–2:45 long, about 3 words a second, at least 2 sound words

## Honest note
No formula guarantees a hit. Gneef has 2.19M subscribers, so their views start from a big audience. What the data does show:
- R6Rants (169K subs) got **6.7M (40× its subscriber count)**.
- Akira's Diary hit 31× its usual average.

So a new channel can break out with this structure. Post often: Gneef has 282 videos.
