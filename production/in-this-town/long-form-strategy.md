# In This Town: long-form-only strategy (deep research, Nov 18, 2026)

**The question:** how to make In This Town long-form videos cheaply, in a way YouTube rewards, and do better than the other channels using the same AI models.

**Sources:**
- YouTube's monetization policy text (support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392).
- vidIQ outliers and keyword data.
- YouTube API data on about 60 videos.
- Frames, descriptions and comments from the leading channels.

---

## 1. The verdict
1. **Don't re-upload anyone's clips, Instagram creators included, even with permission.** YouTube's own rule: reused content *"applies even if you have permission from the original creator"*, and *"the policy applies to the whole channel."* One lazy compilation can demonetize everything.
2. **The real gap is ORIGINAL, NATIVE LONG-FORM:**
   - Almost every In This Town compilation is a re-upload pile of vertical Shorts with blurred sides.
   - That pile is now flooded: compilations from the last few days get 1.4K–28K views, against 500K–2.5M three weeks ago.
   - Those channels will fail the monetization review.
   - The channels winning *and* able to earn compile **their own** stories (FriendBob, Blox Admin, Custerk Blox, Gelo Stories).
   - Nobody makes these **natively in 16:9 for TV screens.**
3. **Production: a cheap hybrid "motion-story".**
   - Consistent characters → AI images for most shots → camera motion, effects and sound on every image → **AI video clips only at the 3 moments that matter** (hook, twist, ending).
   - That's your "one clip at the start, then images" idea, made stronger.
   - It's far cheaper than full animation, and it's allowed: YouTube permits AI when *"the final product shows your own vision."*
4. **Story first.** Your theory is supported by the data:
   - Skull Towns' simple 2D cut-out horror got 1.34M on episode 13.
   - Audio-first horror story channels exist at scale.
   - WhatIfBombo's low-poly (not Roblox) shorts got 4.19M on a 32.9K-sub channel that's 10 months old.
   - **The look must be recognisable and consistent; it doesn't need to be expensive.**

| Rule | What it means for us |
|---|---|

The 10-beat formula is a skeleton, not a fill-in-the-blanks template. Vary the hero, the genre (horror, sad, funny, mystery) and the ending type |

Allowed: AI used when the result "reflects your creative vision and provides… entertainment value" | Our own cast, our own town, our own stories, hand-directed shots. ✅ |
| **Recurring elements are fine:** "same intro and outro… but the bulk of your content is different" | A fixed town, cast, intro and host frame is fine; it's even good for branding |

## 3. Your ideas, checked one by one
| Idea | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Compile reels from Instagram-only creators | ❌ **Never** | Reused content (permission doesn't help) plus copyright. It can kill monetization on the whole channel |
| Mix AI clips that look like the viral videos | ⚠️ **Only in genre, never copies** | The same *genre* and *blocky look* is fine. The same stories, characters or shots is copying, and the "inauthentic content" rule targets look-alikes |
| Characters → AI images → small AI clips → video | ✅ **The recommended path** | Cheap, consistent, scalable, and clearly our own work |
| One AI clip at the start, then images with AI narration | ✅ **Yes, upgraded** | Put clips at the **hook, the twist and the ending** of each story (about 3 clips per story) and give every image motion. Static slideshows lose viewers fast and look "mass-produced" |
| Images only + narration (cheapest) | ⚠️ **Fallback only** | Workable only with strong motion, sound and story. It's the format most at risk of looking templated |
| Roblox style | ✅ **Yes, as our own blocky style** | It's the identity and what people search for. WhatIfBombo proves a **non-Roblox low-poly blocky style** works (4.19M). Don't use Roblox's logo or exact avatars |

## 5. The format to build
**Name idea: "The Town" universe.** One fictional town with a name (for example "Harrowmere", "Blockton", "Stillwater"). One recurring cast. Every rule happens in that same town.
- It's **brand identity** (a viewer remembers "the town").
- It's **easy on the AI**: the same locations and characters get reused, so it's cheaper and more consistent.
- It's **clearly not mass-produced**: a world builds up over time, with callbacks between stories.

**Episode format: native 16:9, 18–25 min, made for TV.**
1. **0:00–0:20 cold open:** the most shocking moment of the episode (an AI clip) + "In this town, there are rules. Break one, and you're gone."
2. **6–8 stories of 2–3 min each,** every one following the 10-beat formula (`hit-formula.md`) but varied in genre and ending.
3. **A short "town map / rulebook" card between stories:** the recurring element, and it keeps viewers watching.
4. **The last story is the strongest,** plus a teaser of next episode's rule (reason to subscribe).
5. **Chapters = each rule as a title.** Every chapter is a mini search hook.

**Collections:**
- Themed: "The Deadliest Rules", "The Saddest Rules", "Rules You'd Break for Love", "Christmas in This Town".
- Seasonal episodes are timely; it's Christmas season now.

**Later:** single long stories (12–18 min) with cliffhangers in parts, the Skull Towns / Gelo model.

**Audience:** teens and adults (thriller, horror, sacrifice, betrayal; no gore, no swearing). Upload as "not made for kids".

## 6. Production pipeline (cheap, and repeatable by the agent)
1. **Story bible (once):** the town name, map, 6–8 recurring characters with fixed looks, locations (main street, school, houses, woods, town hall, rulebook), and the visual style (blocky low-poly, cinematic lighting).
2. **Character reference sheets (once):** generate each character with the Gemini image models, then **reuse those sheets as reference images** in every later prompt. That keeps characters consistent.
3. **Per story (2–3 min):**
   - **Script:** Claude, with the 10-beat checklist.
   - **Shot list:** about 20–30 shots, one every 5–7 s, at 16:9.
   - **Images:** 20–30 per story, generated with the character references.
   - **Clips:** 3 per story (hook, twist, ending), image-to-video from those frames, with whatever AI video tool the owner has (Kling, Veo, Runway or similar).
   - **Motion on every still,** done in Remotion: push-ins, parallax layers, a light camera shake on scares, flicker and fog, rain and snow particles, blink and mouth swaps.
   - **Voice:** the owner's MiniMax narration (or an AI voice), one narrator, first person.
   - **Sound:** SFX at volume 0.025, and written-in sound words.
   - **Captions:** BIG (the owner's rule), bottom-centre.
4. **Episode assembly:**
   - Remotion builds each story from a JSON shot list, then joins 6–8 stories with the cold open, rulebook cards and an end screen.
   - Chapters are generated from story start times.
5. **Cost:**
   - Images cost very little each; video clips are the main cost.
   - About 20–25 clips per episode (3 per story) instead of 150+ is what keeps it cheap.
   - Check each tool's current prices before choosing.

## 7. Search and packaging (copy what works, made better)
- **Title formulas** (keep a search phrase in every title):
  - "In This Town, Every Rule Is DEADLY 😨 | Roblox Stories Compilation" (Blox Admin's working pattern)
  - "8 Towns With 8 Strange Rules | In This Town Roblox Story Compilation"
  - "The Scariest In This Town Stories | Roblox Horror Story Compilation"
- **Description:**
  - Line 1 is a hook question ("11 towns with 11 strange rules. Break one and you're gone. Which rule would you break first? 👇").
  - Then the chapters, then the search phrases, then #roblox #robloxstories #inthistown.
- **Thumbnail** (the proven pattern from Blox Admin and FriendBob): **3 big character faces, each with a BIG rule label** ("DON'T FROWN · WALK SIDEWAYS · DON'T GET FOUND"), or a group shot + "ALL STORIES IN ONE VIDEO!". Bright, readable on a TV.
- **Native 16:9** plus big captions means it looks made *for* long-form, unlike the blurred re-uploads.

## 8. How we beat the others using the same AI models
1. **Original, monetizable** while the copycat compilations get demonetized.
2. **Native 16:9 long-form for TV.** No one else does it.
3. **One town universe:** callbacks, recurring characters, a reason to binge and subscribe.
4. **Better stories:** our formula was studied from 28M–36M-view videos, plus variety in genre and ending (also the policy shield).
5. **Search-first packaging:** a search phrase in every title, chapters per rule, the 3-rule thumbnails.
6. **Volume without sameness:** the cheap hybrid pipeline lets us publish 2 episodes a week with real variety.

## 9. Start here: the test plan
1. **Week 1:**
   - Build the story bible + character sheets.
   - Make **Episode 1** (6 stories, about 18 min).
   - Story 1 can be "Every Lie Turns You Grey", already written.
   - Show the owner stills first.
2. **Weeks 2–4:** two episodes a week, so **7 episodes**.
3. **Watch these numbers** (YouTube Studio, per episode, after 7 days):
   - **Click-through rate:** aim for 5%+.
   - **Average view duration:** aim for 35%+ of the length.
   - **Share of traffic from YouTube search:** it should grow week by week.
4. **Decision at episode 7:**
   - If CTR or retention is weak, fix the thumbnails, cold open or story pacing first.
   - If search traffic is near zero, change the titles toward the exact search phrases.
   - Try one **single long story** episode to compare.

**Honest note:** no format guarantees a hit, and the trend is hot right now (+715%), so speed matters. This plan bets on the one thing the data says lasts: original stories that YouTube can pay for, packaged for search.
