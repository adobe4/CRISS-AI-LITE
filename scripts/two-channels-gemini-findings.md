## Channel 1: Real Strength

### What the pairs proved
| Rule | Proof |
|---|---|
| **One mechanism, never a list.** Go deep on one lever. | Rep Theory: tendons (one idea, 4 holds) 3.4M vs. "12 home gym machines" listicle 4.8K |
| **Underdog identity + a hard number in the first 15 s** | "Skinny guys stronger than their size… tendons 57% stiffer, strength up a third" |
| **Topic must fit the channel's identity** | Same tendon script: 3.4M on Rep Theory ("natural strength" audience) vs. 5K on Blue Gains ("muscle size" audience). **Good news: our Real Strength positioning IS the tendon audience.** |
| **Timeline arc beats a benefits list** | Mr. Well: 20 push-ups Day 1 → Day 2 → Week 2 → Month 2 → Year 10 = 1.15M. Walking as a list of health benefits = 14K |
| **Active challenge > passive habit** | Push-ups 1.15M vs. walking 14K |
| **End with a self-test score** | Rep Theory: "comment your wall-hold time, reply in 4 weeks" |

### Script template (8 min, about 1,250 words at about 155 wpm)
1. **0:00–0:30 · Hook:** an everyday observation ("the skinny guy who wins every arm wrestle") + the hidden cause + one hard number + an open loop ("at the end, the test that tells you where you stand").
2. **0:30–2:00 · Mechanism:** what happens inside on rep 1. One original analogy (not the overused engine/tow-rope).
3. **2:00–4:00 · Timeline:** Day 1 → Week 2 → Month 1 → Month 3. Rook's week counter on screen.
4. **4:00–6:00 · Evidence:** 2–3 named studies (**institution + year on screen**, never "researchers in Germany"). Then the trap: "here's what almost everyone gets wrong".
5. **6:00–7:15 · Copy-It Routine:** exact sets, holds and progression rule, plus a self-test scale.
6. **7:15–8:00 · Close:** a one-line safety note, "comment your score", and a tease of the next video in the series.

### Slop to avoid (Gemini's list)
- the same metaphors everyone uses (engine/tow rope, broomstick, door frame)
- vague citations
- one identical script skeleton every time
- a flat AI voice
- the same character doing nothing new

**Our fix:** Rook visibly changes across the series, every source is named on screen, and we use 3 rotating structures:
- timeline ("What X every day does")
- mechanism ("Why X are stronger")
- myth vs. evidence

## Channel 2: What If

### What the pairs proved
| Rule | Proof |
|---|---|
| **Human stakes beat big-number trivia** | Awaken: "2 strangers must restart humanity" 1.6M vs. "8 billion in one city" 4.3K (from the same channel and voice) |
| **Hook = one sensory scene, never a list of questions** | Hit: "You wake up on a Monday… no traffic, no sirens…" Flop: "Is there room, what would we eat, where would the poop go…" |
| **Emotional rollercoaster** | Hope (the maths works) → horror (inbreeding, the Habsburgs) → hope (nature survived it before) |
| **Plant a detail early that becomes the danger later** | IFOLOGY: "your reflexes stay normal, remember that" (0:24) → stopping-distance crisis (4:30) |
| **End on a personal question, not a summary** | "Who would you want standing next to you on that empty street?" |
| **Recurring characters build a universe** | IFOLOGY ends with a team and a villain cliffhanger → viewers binge |

### Script template (8–9 min)
1. **0:00–0:45 · Inciting moment:** you, mid-scene, the event happens, and the clock starts ("You have 8 minutes"). Plant one detail that matters later.
2. **0:45–2:30 · Honeymoon:** the strange or funny first effects in daily life.
3. **2:30–5:00 · The physics turns:** the planted detail becomes the danger. Use original analogies.
4. **5:00–7:30 · Crisis:** the character's survival hinges on one decision. The Survival Odds meter moves.
5. **7:30–8:30 · Zoom out + personal question:** the final odds score, "what would you do?", and a tease of the character's next scenario.

### Policy note
Morbid curiosity drives clicks, but there must be no gore, suffocation close-ups or panic-death imagery. Danger is shown through the meter and the environment, never through bodies. "Distressing content" is one of the three demonetization types.
