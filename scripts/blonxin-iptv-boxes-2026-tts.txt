PART 2 OF 9 - WHAT ACTUALLY MATTERS

Before we talk about models, here's what actually matters in an IPTV box. Forget the marketing on the packaging. Four things.

One. RAM. Two gigabytes is the bare minimum. Three or four is where everything feels smooth, especially with big channel lists.

Two. Storage. Thirty-two gigabytes gives you room for your player app, updates, and recordings, without running out after a month.

Three. Connection. Wi-Fi six, or even better, an ethernet port. Live TV hates weak Wi-Fi more than anything else.

Four. And this is the one most people miss. Certified Google TV or Android TV. A certified box gets security updates and the official Play Store. An uncertified box often runs a modified system that never gets updated at all. Remember that, because it comes back in the hidden section.

Now the hidden tier. Let's start with the gems.

And now, the part sellers don't want you to know. The hidden traps.

Trap number one. The fake spec box. You've seen them online. Eight K. Thirty-two gigabytes of RAM. Two hundred and fifty-six gigabytes of storage. All for thirty dollars. Those numbers are usually fake. The system is modified to show specs the hardware doesn't actually have. In real use, these boxes lag, overheat, and many of them never get a single security update.

PART 8 OF 9 - THE BUFFERING FIX

Now the fix I promised. Whatever box you choose, buffering usually comes from the same three things.

One. Use ethernet if your box has a port. If it doesn't, connect to the five gigahertz Wi-Fi band, not two point four.

Two. Inside your player app, find the buffer setting and raise it. Most apps ship with it set too low for live TV.

Three. Switch the video player or decoder inside the app. If a channel stutters on the default player, the alternative player often fixes it instantly.

Those three changes fix most buffering problems I see, before you ever need to blame your provider.
